Shot Curve Basics

When a player shoots, the ball flies in a curve shaped like a hill. Maths has a name for that curve and an equation for it. Here you will see why, and then use it to work out how to make a shot go in.

  • h height of the ball when it leaves the hand
  • d distance from the player to the hoop
  • u sideways speed of the ball
  • w upward speed of the ball at the start

Step 1

Two motions at the same time

A flying ball does two things at once. Sideways it keeps a steady speed. Up and down, gravity slows it on the way up and speeds it on the way down. Drag the time slider and watch the two shadows.

The dots are 0.1 s apart. On the floor they are evenly spaced, so the sideways speed is steady. On the left they bunch together near the top, because the ball is slow there.

Step 2

Where the quadratic comes from

Write each motion as a formula. Here t is the time in seconds, and we use g = 10 m/s² for gravity, a little rounded.

  1. Sideways: distance equals speed times time.

    x = u t

  2. Height: start at h, go up at w, and gravity takes away 5t². The 5 is half of g.

    y = h + w t − 5t²

  3. From the first formula, t = x / u. Put that into the second formula. Time disappears, and you get the curve itself.

    y = h + wu x − 5u² x²

  4. This has the form y = c + bx + ax², a quadratic. The three numbers mean:

    c = h   b = w / u   a = −5 / u²
    • c is the start height.
    • b is the steepness: metres up for every metre sideways at the start.
    • a is the bend. It is negative, so the curve is a hill. A faster sideways speed makes it smaller and the hill flatter.

Step 4

Make it go in

Instead of guessing, use the equation. Choose how steep you want the shot, and the maths gives the sideways speed that hits the hoop. It uses the distance and release height from Step 3.

Put x = d and y = 3 into the curve:   3 = h + bd − 5d²u²
Rearrange for u:   u = √5d²h + bd − 3   and then   w = b × u

Step 5

How hard do you push?

The curve needs the speed at release. To get that speed, the hand pushes the ball for a short time. Use F = m × a, where the acceleration is the speed gained divided by the push time.

The ball has a mass of about 0.6 kg. This is an average force, and the push times are example values, not measurements.
