Five structural challenges are impeding programme velocity. Each is addressable — but all require leadership decisions, not just recruiting effort. Expand each challenge to see the full diagnostic.
01
Headcount approval friction
HR sign-off delayed across multiple markets simultaneously
✓ Largely resolved
Problem
Multiple requests submitted simultaneously, creating a volume surge HR was not structured to process
Approval chains vary by country — local HR, regional HRBP, and finance sign-off required in different sequences
Roles classified as "new" triggered additional budget committee reviews
Root cause
No pre-agreed multi-market headcount plan — each request processed in isolation
A2o Consultant is a new category with no existing budget envelope or workforce plan
HR lacked context on business urgency behind simultaneous multi-market timing
Mitigation
Wave-based programme plan now in place — submit remaining approvals as a single consolidated Wave 2/3 request
Executive sponsorship secured — Wave 1 hires provide precedent and authority for subsequent approvals
Dedicated HRBP engagement in progress for multi-market routing
02
Job description and grade misalignment
Hybrid role doesn't fit standard HR job architecture
⏱ Ongoing
Problem
No HR template combining AI/LLM, Genesys, and senior consulting in a single grade
JD flagged as "too technical" for consulting band or "too commercial" for engineering band
Comp benchmarking failed — no comparable role in salary survey data, causing pay range disputes
Root cause
Role sits at intersection of three disciplines historically treated as separate career tracks
HR architecture designed for established functional roles, not emerging hybrid profiles
Lack of external data defaults comp to the lower band, making offers uncompetitive
Mitigation
Create "Sr. Principal A2o Consultant" and "A2o Consultant" as distinct job families anchored to consulting band
Commission blended comp benchmarking using Wave 1 offer data alongside Radford / Mercer external data
Publish one globally consistent JD per grade with local language variants — eliminates per-market JD renegotiation
03
Talent scarcity — the tri-skill gap
AI + Genesys + enterprise consulting in one profile is rare
⚠ Critical
Problem
Standard sourcing returns AI engineers with no client skills or CCaaS consultants with no AI depth — rarely both
Genesys-certified professionals are a small global community; those with agentic AI project experience are a fraction
Fully-qualified candidates are in senior roles, not actively looking, and receive multiple competing offers when they surface
Root cause
Profile only became commercially relevant in the last 2–3 years — market hasn't yet produced a significant cohort
Genesys expertise is concentrated in a small set of SI partners (TTEC, Concentrix, Accenture CXaaS) and rarely circulates outside them
AI consulting is split: hyperscaler alumni lack CCaaS depth; Genesys specialists haven't made the AI leap yet
Mitigation
Adopt "2 of 3 skills + structured 90-day upskill" as the formal hiring bar — expands addressable pool 3–5×
Target Genesys SI partner ecosystem directly — TTEC, Concentrix, and Accenture CXaaS alumni are the densest available pool
Build a warm talent community — maintain active relationships with qualified candidates not yet ready to move
04
Recruiter pipeline quality
Agencies returning keyword matches, not genuine hybrid profiles
↗ Mitigating
Problem
Recruitment partners returned candidates matching individual keywords rather than the hybrid profile
Generic recruiters lacked domain fluency — candidates passed HR screen but failed competency interviews
Unsuitable shortlists slowed programme velocity and created hiring manager frustration
Root cause
Standard briefing documents don't convey the nuance of an emerging hybrid role — recruiters default to existing databases
No specialist recruiter has a ready-made pool — this profile requires proactive market mapping, not database search
Recruiter incentives tied to placement volume — without tight qualification criteria, speed takes precedence over fit
Mitigation — in progress
Leveraging own and partner networks directly — bypassing generalist recruiters; warm introductions yield significantly higher-quality profiles
Engaging Genesys partner ecosystem as a structured sourcing channel — partner referrals outperform cold agency outreach on fit
Mandatory pre-screen scoring rubric introduced — only candidates meeting 2 of 3 skill criteria reach hiring manager stage
05
European notice periods — 2–3 months
Hire decisions today translate to Q4 starts — every delay compounds
⏱ Structural
Problem
Standard European employment contracts carry 2–3 month notice periods for senior roles — the time-to-start is structurally longer than time-to-hire
A successful hire made in July will not be in seat until October at earliest — this is not a process failure, it is market reality
Each week of delay in making a hire decision pushes the start date one week further — compounding impact across 5 active roles in Wave 2
Root cause
European labour law and senior employment norms — notice periods of 2–3 months are standard for this level across UK, Germany, Netherlands, Italy, and France
Leadership planning timelines have not always accounted for notice period lag — resource availability dates have been underestimated
Candidates with shorter notice periods are rare and often less experienced — notice period length is a signal of seniority, not a negotiating point
Mitigation
Rebuild all hiring timelines with notice period baked in — communicate Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 start dates as realistic expectations, not failures
Accelerate hire decisions for UK and Germany immediately — every week of pipeline delay now equals a week of delayed revenue capacity in Q4
For Wave 3 (Dubai, South Africa), initiate hiring now so that expansion roles are in seat by Q1 2027 — these markets also carry notice period obligations

Approval to in-seat — minimum
⏱
The total pipeline from approval to consultant in seat is a minimum of 4–5 months. The hiring process itself cannot be compressed below 6–8 weeks, and European notice periods add a further 2–3 months on top. This is not a process problem — it is the structural reality of hiring senior professionals in European markets. Every week of delay in making a hire decision today pushes the productive start date one week further into 2027.
Where each stage is breaking down
🔒
Pre-funnel — 2-week posting window
Fixed dead time before sourcing can begin. Only mitigation: warm candidates through networks before the role goes public. Relationship building requires no live posting.
🔍
Top of funnel — pool scarcity
The market does not have enough of this profile. Generalist recruiters return keyword matches. Active sourcing via own networks and the Genesys partner ecosystem is the only reliable path.
📅
Post-hire — notice period lag
2–3 months from offer acceptance to Day 1 in seat. This is not negotiable for senior European hires. Revenue capacity impact must be planned around this constraint, not against it.
The levers that actually move this
🌐
Increase top-of-funnel — the primary lever
More candidates at the top improves every stage below. Own networks + partner ecosystem + pre-screened "2 of 3 skill" candidates. Network sourcing can begin before the posting window opens — this is the single most impactful action available.
⏱
Submit approvals earlier — run windows in parallel
Submitting Wave 3 approvals now means the posting clock runs concurrently with Wave 2 active hiring. Earlier submission is the only way to compress the total programme timeline against the notice period constraint.
👥
Always carry a reserve to final stage
Never run a single-candidate final. A declined offer or rejection resets the process by 3–4 weeks — plus the full notice period. Running two candidates in parallel costs one extra interview but eliminates restart risk entirely.
🔧
Formally accept the "2 of 3 skills" hiring bar
A structured 90-day upskill programme expands the addressable candidate pool by 3–5× immediately. Perfect candidates are vanishingly rare and already employed. The constraint is bar-setting, not market size.
💡
The 2-week posting window is also worth a direct policy question to HR. It likely exists for internal mobility priority and equal-opportunity compliance. Worth confirming: does it apply uniformly across all 8 markets? Are there exceptions for hard-to-fill specialist roles? Can the window run concurrently with the approval process? That last question alone could recover a week per hire across the full programme.
