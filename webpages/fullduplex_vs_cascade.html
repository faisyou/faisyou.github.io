01 — The concept

Full duplex is a telecom term, borrowed for voice AI

In telecommunications, a full-duplex channel lets both parties send and receive at the same time — like a phone call. A half-duplex channel forces one direction at a time — like a walkie-talkie, where you have to say "over" before the other person can respond.

Applied to voice AI, a full-duplex model is a single system that continuously listens while it is generating speech. It never fully hands off the microphone. It can be interrupted mid-sentence, it can yield gracefully, and it can backchannel ("mhmm," a nod-equivalent) without an external component telling it when to do so.

Most consumer voice assistants people have used to date — including the original ChatGPT voice mode — are not this. They are cascaded pipelines dressed up to feel conversational.

The typical voice agent today: three models in a relay

Speech-to-Text → Large Language Model → Text-to-Speech, with a voice-activity detector (VAD) deciding when the caller has "finished talking."

Each hop adds latency, and a VAD module has to guess when silence means "done" versus "thinking." This is why cascaded bots often talk over a hesitant pause, or wait too long after a quick reply.

The full-duplex agent: one continuous stream

🎙 ⇄ 🔊   one model, one continuous audio stream, listening and speaking concurrently No handoff between components. No VAD deciding when to speak. Interruption is a native behavior, not a bolted-on feature.
02 — The landscape

NVIDIA and OpenAI have both arrived at full duplex, but from different directions and for different audiences. OpenAI's earlier GPT-4o Realtime voice mode was speech-native but still leaned on a turn detector — genuinely full duplex only arrived with GPT-Live in July 2026.

03 — Trade-offs

Full duplex vs. the cascaded pipeline

Full duplex (end-to-end)

NVIDIA VoiceChat · GPT-Live · Moshi · Gemini Live
  • +Sub-second, natural turn-taking; genuine barge-in and backchanneling
  • +No brittle voice-activity-detector guessing when the caller is "done"
  • +Preserves tone, hesitation, and emotion instead of flattening speech to text first
  • +Fewer moving parts to orchestrate in production (one model, not three services)
  • –Narrower language coverage — see focus below
  • –Weaker reasoning depth — see focus below
  • –Harder to bolt on tool-calling, RAG, and guardrails; vendors are still inventing workarounds (side channels, delegation)
  • –Immature commercial tooling; NVIDIA's flagship model is research-labeled and self-hosted only

Cascaded (STT → LLM → TTS)

Classic voice-bot stack, including newer VAD-free variants
  • +Full reasoning power of frontier text LLMs — nothing traded away for speech
  • +Mature, swappable components; easy tool-calling, RAG, logging, compliance review
  • +Broad language and dialect coverage from established ASR/TTS vendors
  • +Cheaper to run and easier to debug at scale
  • –Extra latency at every hop (transcribe, reason, synthesize)
  • –Loses prosody and emotional nuance once speech becomes flat text
  • –Classic versions feel stilted, waiting for silence before responding
  • –Interruption handling is bolted on, not native
04 — The trade-off that matters most for enterprise deployment
what the demos don't show you

Full duplex buys naturalness — at the cost of languages and intelligence

The turn-taking numbers are genuinely impressive. But two constraints are easy to miss in a vendor demo, and both matter directly for any multilingual, reasoning-heavy deployment.

Why languages lag

End-to-end full-duplex models must learn listening and speaking jointly from dual-channel conversational audio — a data type that is scarce even in English and far scarcer in most other languages.

NVIDIA's flagship model uses a speech encoder explicitly trained for English. The newest multilingual full-duplex benchmark, M3-DuplexBench, currently covers just two languages (English and Japanese) — that is the frontier of academic evaluation today.

Cascaded pipelines don't have this problem: each leg (ASR, TTS) is a separately-trained, mature component — Whisper-class ASR and modern TTS engines already span well over 100 languages and dialects, because they were never required to learn speech generation and conversational timing at once.

Why intelligence lags

Academic surveys of these architectures note that full-duplex end-to-end models typically expose only speech as output and trade away some of the text-reasoning strength that pure LLMs have, in exchange for low-latency conversational behavior.

OpenAI's own product decision is the clearest evidence of this: rather than making GPT-Live itself the reasoner, it keeps a lightweight voice-native model on the live audio path and asynchronously delegates harder questions to GPT-5.5 in the background. If the full-duplex layer were reasoning-equivalent to a frontier LLM, that delegation step wouldn't be necessary.

05 — Bottom line

What this means for a deployment decision

01

For multilingual, reasoning-heavy contact-center use cases — think Arabic/English dual-language support, complex billing disputes, or multi-step troubleshooting — a cascaded (or VAD-free cascaded) pipeline remains the safer choice today. It keeps full LLM reasoning and broad language coverage intact.

02

Full duplex is the right bet where naturalness and latency are the product — companion-style assistants, high-volume English-language front doors, or as a conversational "front end" layered over a stronger reasoning model via delegation, the way GPT-Live now does.

03

Expect the gap to close, not stay fixed. Delegation patterns (route hard reasoning to a bigger model) and hybrid VAD-free cascades are both explicit attempts to buy full-duplex naturalness without giving up intelligence or language breadth — that convergence point is the one to watch for the next 12 months.
